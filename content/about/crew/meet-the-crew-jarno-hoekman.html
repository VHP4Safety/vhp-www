Jarno Hoekman, MSc, PhD, is an associate professor in Innovation Studies at Utrecht University. His research focuses on understanding and studying the governance and dynamics of science in the context of innovation, regulation and societal challenges, with a particular focus on the life-sciences domain. He is particularly interested in how scientific evidence informs regulatory decision-making and how new tests and standards to assess the safety of life-sciences innovations are developed and institutionalised.




Jarno and his colleagues (Ellen Moors, Simona Negro, Wouter Boon, Tessel Wijne and Marjolein Hoogstraaten) involved in VHP4Safety introduce themselves in this video:

What is your role in the VHP4Safety project?

“I’m involved in Research Line 3. Specifically, I am Work Package Leader for WP 3.1, and involved in the more general research of WP 3.2. WP 3.1 is about technology assessment of the Virtual Human Platform. Our focus is on understanding and steering the process of building, and later on implementing, the platform. We’re studying how over time different stakeholders are engaged, but we’re also working on clarifying ideas about what the platform should look like, from the point of view of end-users as well as researchers in the project. At the moment, we’re at the stage of trying to answer questions like ‘What is a platform actually?’ and ‘What does it mean for different researchers?’. Different people may have a very different idea of what a platform actually entails: is it at its core social, or technical and how are the two combined? While delving into these questions, we make sure to involve end-users from the start.”

How is your work interconnected with the other Research Lines?

“Actually, looking for that interconnection, trying to understand expectations and ambitions of researchers involved in the project is one of our goals. Once we clarify those, we want to establish whether these expectations align with what stakeholders need and prefer. At this stage, it might be about basic design principles: how can you ensure transparency? How can you ensure that the platform is developed in such a way that it is still relatively open and can adapt to emerging insights or new technologies in the future? If those principles are leading, the next step is about clarifying what these principles entail concretely, and how that aligns with how stakeholders can be involved and use the platform.”

What were your main takeaways from the workshop on 22 November?

“In WP 3.2 we’re working on mapping out the ecosystem around the project. We identify relevant stakeholders and which place they take in the ecosystem, from being involved in research and development in safety assessment based on human data, all the way to a situation where this might be actually realized in regulatory and industry practice. We try to design what the innovation process would look like, and what stakeholders would be important to engage and to involve. One of the key takeaways for me is that everyone is searching, including the stakeholders and the researchers. Our task is to systematize that search a bit and to come up with more stylized ideas of what this process would look like and how we could intervene or guide it.”

When would you be satisfied with the project?

“This project provides enormous momentum to contribute to a broader transition to animal-free safety assessment. That transition needs to be realized in all kinds of different arenas: science, regulation, policy, markets . It all needs to be brought together. I think VHP4Safety really has the potential of being that connecting piece of the puzzle, specifically in the Dutch context. In the research proposal, it says that the Dutch ecosystem will be a global frontrunner in animal-free safety assessment based on human data. I’m excited to be contributing to that goal with this project, but also mindful we are just part of the solution to a major societal challenge, so I suppose some degree of modesty would be wise.

Has anything struck you while working on this project?

“Even though we haven’t seen each other live very often, the general atmosphere in the project strikes me as very open. Everyone is open to explore and share ideas. People are not judgmental. There is an open, interdisciplinary mindset. The people we have on board really embrace this multifaceted nature of VHP4Safety, or at least attempt to do so. We are still all searching, but we’re doing it together.”

Tell us something not many people know about you

“I’m quite a fanatic cyclist. To me, cycling is a great way to decompress from the stresses of everyday life. When I go on holidays, I take the racing bike along. I really like to explore, so apart from the well-known mountains like the Ventoux or Alpe d'Huez, I like to discover roads less travelled. With a group of friends, I once cycled from the coast of the Atlantic Ocean in France all the way to the Mediterranean Sea, through the Pyrenees. We did it in five days and I can tell you it wasn’t easy, so it felt like quite an achievement and it was a really memorable experience.”
