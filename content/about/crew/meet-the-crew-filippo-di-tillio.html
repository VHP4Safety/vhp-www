Tell us a bit about yourself
My name is Filippo, I am originally from Italy and I moved to the Netherlands in January to start as a PhD candidate at Leiden University. My background is in mathematics. I studied in Italy and got my bachelor's degree at the University of L’Aquila. For my master's degree I specialized in applied mathematics. I did the first year of my master's degree in Italy as well, and then I continued and finished the degree in Sweden. I did this mainly because in Italy the education is rather theoretical, and in Northern Europe the focus is more on the applied, more practical part of the field. I wanted to explore that side as well and as it turns out, I really like it.

What is your role in VHP4Safety?
I am involved in WP1.3, where I do computational modelling for the biology that is relevant for the project. I predict adverse outcomes in for instance kidney nephrotoxicity. I'm working on quantitative adverse outcome pathway models, QAOP's, with my colleagues Joost Beltman and Bob van de Water.

I find the AOP strategy fascinating because you start with a very simple scheme, and then combine different linear schemes to create a much more complicated network, with the aim of describing the biology that is relevant for the problem you're analyzing. I'm currently creating a framework, which we can use to list the key biological events and dive deeper into each of these. The aim is to describe the dynamics of the problem at different time points to see how the key events connect to each other and to describe the dynamics of the key event itself.

What about applying mathematical models to biology interests you?
In math everything is black and white, or right or wrong, so what fascinates me about biology is that there are always two sides to the coin. For instance, I studied DNA damage. At first glance, that seems to be bad for your body, but there are chemotherapeutic agents that act through DNA damage so in that case DNA damage is not purely a bad thing. It’s fascinating to me that we can apply black and white mathematics to these more complicated biological problems. What I also like about diving deeper into biology is that I am learning more about how our body works.

What's your experience living and working in the Netherlands?
What I like about Dutch culture is that when people are at work, they work hard and efficiently, taking only short breaks. That means that when the work day is done, there's time left to do other things you enjoy. Apart from that, I suppose the stereotype rings true: things in the Netherlands are more organized than in Italy.

What do you look forward to in the VHP4Safety project?
Meeting all the different people involved in the project. In such a big project, it's a challenge to find each other and share knowledge. There's a useful overview in Teams of all participants per WP, so that helps. And Tessel Wijne, another PhD student in the project, created a platform for all young professionals within VHP4Safety to exchange knowledge and experiences. I look forward to many interesting exchanges, on screen if necessary, but also face-to-face whenever possible.
