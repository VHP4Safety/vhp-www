Juliette wears many hats in our VHP4Safety project. Together with Anne Kienhuis and Cyrille Krul, Juliette is coordinator of VHP4Safety. But she's also leader of Research Line 2 and directly involved in the science, along with her colleagues from the UU/IRAS toxicology division (see picture). In this interview, we’re getting to know Juliette a bit better.




Juliette, what was your personal motivation to start up this project?

“Well, I’m a toxicologist and my research has been focusing on determining the effects of chemicals on humans, animals and the environment for many years now. Working on alternatives to laboratory animals has also been a long-term focus of mine. I’ve done a lot of work with zebrafish as an alternative to higher vertebrate species. Finding a better way to understand the health impact of chemical exposures has really motivated me from early on.

When I came to Utrecht and heard about the TPI (Transition to Animal-free Innovations), it really struck me how important this issue is in Utrecht. U-Aim is another example of that. It's a hub on advanced in-vitro models with researchers across the university. From the moment I've been based in Utrecht, the topic of reducing and replacing animals in toxicology has been a really important one.”

When will VHP4Safety be a success to you?

“It actually already feels like a success! We have a lot of work to do, but the fact that we've brought together all these different parties and stakeholders, with different interests and different backgrounds, is such an achievement already. Every little step in this whole chain of safety assessment, from the scientists, to the policymakers, to the chemical manufacturers, to the consumer organizations, they are all on board! Including social scientists, who are helping us to better understand how we can make this transition accepted by the wider public. That’s so exciting and inspiring.

Apart from that, for me, the project will be a success if we manage to build this platform and actually use it. Having this platform of data and databases together and working in a way that we can actually predict the risk of exposure to chemicals. If we finish this project and the platform is sustainable and being used, that is the ultimate success. The last thing we want to do is spend five years building something that won’t be used. The long-term implementation of this new paradigm is really important.”

What has struck you recently while working on this project?

“I think what is really special about this project is that we are trying to co-create from day one. I have never been involved in a project like this one. We have sketched the contours of the project, but we don’t have a definitive plan. We are really building it together from the start. We are trying to involve as many different parties as possible, so that our concept will be accepted from the start. That makes it very special, but maybe also a bit scary to me. I have coordinated other projects before, but those always have a very clear plan and very clear deliverables. Of course you may deviate from the plan a bit as you go forward, but for the most part, a project is clear and straight-forward and people know what they are doing. The disadvantage of that is that you risk people working in isolation. You may meet all your milestones and deliverables, but you won’t have a big, integrated, innovative project that takes things to the next level. And that is what VHP4Safety is all about. We are designing, executing and implementing as we go along. That requires a lot of discussion and a lot of time and a different way of thinking. That’s exciting, but also quite different.

I also really enjoy working together with my co-coordinators Cyrille and Anne and our project manager Laura. They have impressive long-term scientific experience and an amazing mindset that is so exciting and new for me. I just love working with them! Normally I am used to coordinating on my own, but now, with our coordination team, we make the decisions together, and that makes VHP4Safety a really dynamic and constantly growing project.”

Now on to some more ‘light’ matters: what's the last thing that made you laugh out loud?

“Good question! I've had a challenging and busy few days, but it's so important to keep smiling and laughing. My six-month-old chocolate Labrador definitely makes me laugh a lot. His name is Mando, named after a Star Wars character. I also have two teenage kids at home who are constantly making me laugh.

Busy or not, there's always a reason to laugh, every day. The other day, I was in a course on leadership and change management and we were asked to do a little exercise, where we had to change five things about our appearance and the other person had to guess what they were. We had to keep taking turns changing more things. After a few turns, you have to get very, very creative with what to change next, so it was hilarious. Until somebody said: ‘I don’t like this anymore, let’s stop this exercise!’ Turned out that was actually the point of the exercise: too much change in too short a timeframe is frustrating to people. Perhaps that's a good lesson for us at VHP4Safety too. Change is needed, but we need to take it step by step. And keep smiling along the way!”
