Hi Mirthe! Can you tell us a bit about yourself?

Mirthe recently graduated from the University of Applied Sciences Utrecht, where she studied Biology and Medical Laboratory Research. She specialised in bioinformatics and data science, but her path into tech started even earlier.

“Before that, I actually studied Communication & Multimedia Design. I quickly realised I enjoyed the technical side of things – especially building websites and working with LLMs’.”

That combination of scientific and technical skills still defines her work today. What drives her most is helping others understand complex topics.

“I want to make complicated things as simple and understandable as possible. Whether that’s through design, research, presentations or diagrams - that’s what I enjoy most.”

She sees a key role for herself in helping bridge the gap between different disciplines, and making sure insights aren’t lost in translation.

“I’ve noticed that experts, also in this project, can dive so deep into their own field that it becomes hard to explain to others. I really enjoy making that information accessible, and seeing people take pride in understanding it.”

When did you get involved in the VHP4Safety project?

Mirthe started working on the project in September 2023 as part of her graduation internship, which focused on automating PBPK models. After graduating in June 2024, she officially joined the team.

“I’m employed by Maastricht University now, but I still work closely with the University of Applied Sciences Utrecht. I move a bit between the two, and those groups collaborate a lot anyway.”

What is your role in the project?

Mirthe works across different parts of the project, and she likes it that way. She’s both IT developer (focused on the platform’s user interface and user experience) and a data scientist for PBPK models.

“When I work with toxicological data, I understand the technical constraints. And now that I work more on the platform side, I also better understand the toxicology perspective and how to make things work for them.”

She also likes to pick up tasks wherever she can.

“I have a hands-on mindset. I like getting things done.”

You’ve recently started working in an agile way within Research Line 1. How’s that going?

“Really well!” she says. “It fits the way I already try to work - with structure, regular check-ins and clear goals.”

She’s also noticed how much better the communication has become between different parts of the project.

“People are less on their own little islands now. There’s more awareness of what others are working on, and where we can support each other.”

It’s also how the need for the web development became clear.

What do you find most interesting about the project?

Mirthe loves the variety of expertise in the consortium, and the shared commitment to reducing animal testing.

“I once had to dissect a rat as part of my studies, and it felt awful. That’s when I decided I wanted to help reduce animal testing as part of my career.”

That decision eventually led her to Marc Teunis and to her current role in VHP4Safety.

She’s also drawn to the ambition of the project.

“I really like that we’re building workflows - that we’re trying to bring everything together, instead of just developing separate tools.”

During our recent Designathon, you led a session on the user experience. How are the outcomes of that session being implemented?

“It was such a useful session, I’m really happy with what came out of it.”

Mirthe made a to-do list from all the input, and is already implementing many of the smaller suggestions.

“Some things are really easy to adjust, like adding a short description somewhere.”

Bigger discussion points, like how workflows are shown, how we present and share data, and how to implement in vitro models on the platform, still need follow-up.

“We’re also restarting the data management task force, and we’ll work more closely with Research Line 2 on the workflows. Jente is already working on templates for that.”

The goal is to have the first interactive version of the platform - a clickable demo - ready for the next hackathon.

You also co-organised workshop on making tutorial videos. When can we expect your first one? 😉

“I’ve made videos before - not for this project yet, but as part of my previous studies. I really like using videos to explain complex things in a simple way.”

For Mirthe, making a video is also a learning process.

“I think the best way to learn something is to explain it to someone else. So if I want to really understand something, I make a video about it.”

So far, she hasn’t published any - partly due to perfectionism - but she’s excited to start.

And what topic would you like to cover in your first VHP video?

“Probably RO-Crates - that would make the most sense and be the most relevant for the project right now. Or maybe something about PBPK models.”

And lastly, what do you enjoy doing in your free time?

Mirthe spends a lot of time outdoors with her partner - hiking, kayaking, climbing. She’s also a gamer, loves her two cats, and enjoys making jewellery.

And she likes programming fun side projects in her spare time.
