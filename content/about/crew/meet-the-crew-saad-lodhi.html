Hi Saad! Can you tell us a little bit about yourself?
Saad Lodhi is a PhD candidate at Maastricht University, working on transcriptomics tool development within the VHP4Safety project. Originally from Lahore, Pakistan, he has an academic background in biotechnology and systems biology, and brings both computational and biological expertise to the team.

“I did my Bachelor’s in biotechnology in Lahore,” he says. “For my Master’s, I moved to Sweden to study systems biology at the University of Skövde. That was quite a climate change!”

As part of his Master's programme, Saad carried out a research project at the Karolinska Institute, where he later worked as a research assistant on autoimmune disorders. He then took on a role at Unilabs back in Skövde, analysing COVID-19 samples during the third wave of the pandemic. “It was an intense period,” he recalls. “And although I learned a lot, including the Swedish language, I realised I wanted to return to research more aligned with my background.”

That decision brought him to the Netherlands, where he now lives in Heerlen with his wife and son. “We couldn’t find a suitable place in Maastricht, but Heerlen is close by and it works well for us.”

When did you get involved in the VHP4Safety project?
Interestingly, Saad originally applied for a PhD position within the ONTOX project. “There were two final candidates,” he says. “And I was offered the opportunity to work on VHP4Safety instead, which sounded just as interesting, if not more. The idea of building a risk assessment platform felt very relevant and forward-looking.”

What is your role in the project?

Within VHP4Safety, Saad is active in Work Packages 1.1 and 1.3, focusing primarily on transcriptomics analysis. “My work is on the development side,” he says. “I build tools that help analyse transcriptomics data, specifically for integration into the platform.”

He works closely with Marvin, Egon, and his co-promotor Danyel at Maastricht University, and also collaborates with Ivo on machine learning and AI applications.

A key challenge in his work has been access to relevant data. “We don’t generate our own transcriptomics datasets in this project, so we rely on external sources. Unfortunately, people are often hesitant to share raw data before they’ve published it themselves — even if we’re not planning to publish on it, just use it in tool development.”

What are you currently working on?

Despite the data challenges, Saad is currently collaborating with Marvin on a case study related to kidney toxicity. “We’re working with an analysis pipeline based on R-ODAF, developed here in Maastricht. Normally, running the full analysis requires coding, but I’ve developed a Shiny app so that users can run it via a web interface, without needing coding skills.”

The aim is to integrate this transcriptomics tool with AOP-related tools already developed in the project. “We want to demonstrate a case study using compounds like tacrolimus and cisplatin to show how transcriptomics and AOPs can be linked in practical safety assessment.”

What do you find most interesting about the project?
For Saad, the most rewarding part of VHP4Safety is the collaborative environment. “I’ve learned a lot from colleagues here – about AOPs, about AI, about risk assessment in general. Everyone brings something different to the table.”

You recently visited the Eurotox conference in Athens. Can you tell us more about it?

Saad presented a poster at EUROTOX 2025 in Athens. “It was a great experience. I was surprised by how many posters focused on AOPs — there’s clearly growing momentum in this area.”

His own poster was on comparing transcriptomics data from mouse and human models of gastrointestinal toxicity caused by doxorubicin. “But my main goal was really to network and explore whether we could access datasets relevant to our work.”

And finally, what do you enjoy doing in your free time?
Outside of work, Saad enjoys reading, walking, going to the gym, and driving. “I don’t have a lot of free time these days with a young child,” he says with a smile. “But I recently took a long drive to Switzerland. I really enjoyed the beautiful landscapes.”
