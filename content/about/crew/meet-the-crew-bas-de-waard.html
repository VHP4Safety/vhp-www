Hi Bas! Can you tell us a bit more about your role in the project?
Within VHP4Safety I am involved in stakeholder engagement. I am seconded from the funder ZonMw and one day a week I work for VHP4Safety. I fulfil this role alongside my position as program manager on the program 'More knowledge with fewer animals'. The VHP4Safety project obviously fits perfectly with this and I really see opportunity for a synergistic cooperation here. On the one hand, from the experience I gained at ZonMw I have a large network, and I have insight into other initiatives and developments within this field. In this way, I hope to be able to contribute to and speed up the implementation of the project. On the other hand, within this consortium there is a lot of expertise in the field of animal-free innovations, which can help me a lot in my work. In this way, I hope to strengthen synergy within the consortium and with other relevant initiatives and stakeholders in the field, and identify strategic opportunities for the project. At the same time the vast expertise that exists in the consortium can give invaluable insights for myself and the policies of ZonMw.

That’s why I want to stress that I am not involved as a funder per se, but as a new member of the consortium and involved in the project itself. With my expertise in stakeholder engagement, I hope to get more stakeholders excited and thus increase the impact of the project. I joined the consortium in September and have already been involved in the preparation of the stakeholder meeting that will take place on November 18. I hope to get the stakeholders more involved and enthusiastic there, and I really like that I get to know more and more people in the consortium in this way.

Why is the project of interest to you personally?
Mainly because I really like the goal and the approach of the project. It really shows an enormous ambition. To change the procedure of safety assessment to one purely based on human data and without the use of animals requires a coordinated effort on multiple fronts: technical, scientific and societal. The fact that this is all combined in one project and the enthusiasm of everyone to work together on this really impresses me. During my work at ZonMw, I learned about the complexity of replacing animals in testing: besides the technical and scientific challenge of developing and applying novel approach methodologies (NAMs), you also have to deal with important factors like regulations that need to be changed, and trust in these models needs to be built among users. This complex interplay is addressed in this project and highlights the innovative approach. This, combined with the end goal of making safety assessment of chemicals more efficient and relevant for humans really makes me enthusiastic to work on the project.


When is the project a success for you?
As I just mentioned, I really like the goal of this project and hope that our platform will eventually be fully implemented and used. But what I also think is very important, is the perception of society. I think many people do not have a representative image of laboratory animals and assume that the results of studies with laboratory animals can just be safely applied to humans. However, there is always a degree of uncertainty with laboratory animal use because, after all, an animal is not a human being. It would be nice if this project reignites the discussion and society starts thinking about the question "What do we accept in terms of degree of safety and uncertainty?".

Of course, we must also be honest and transparent about NAMs. After all, we cannot predict with one hundred percent certainty what happens in the human body with NAMs either. The focus should be on what model is best for what research question. It would be a great success for me if the project would prove that NAMs are in fact able to provide a robust safety assessment and that a different approach is possible, without the use of animals. In my view, the discussion for instance in politics is centered too much around how to decrease the number of animals used in research. The question should be: how can we perform science that is as relevant to humans as possible? And to challenge scientists to do just that.


What has struck you recently while working on the project?
What struck me most is the complexity of the project. To get the big picture from all these different levels and set up a virtual human platform requires a lot of collaboration, and I admire the extent to which that is happening within this project. What also strikes me very much is the enormous enthusiasm for the common goal. The enthusiasm is growing not only among the researchers, but also among stakeholders in the field as a whole and that is great to see. There is this common goal that we are working towards, and the different views, expertise and input the stakeholders and scientists have on this topic is a real strength.


What is something not many people know about you?
I really enjoy growing my own peppers every year. I absolutely love sambals and spicy sauces, so I thought: why don't I grow my own peppers? Every winter I start growing them under these special lights. From all those different pepper varieties, I then make my own sambal. My colleagues at ZonMw already know this, because they get a jar of homemade sambal every year. I also got my brother enthusiastic about growing peppers, and I would love to set up a company with him one day and market our own, home-grown sambal.

Anything else you would like to share?
If you want to meet me, discuss something or if you see an opportunity for cooperation, I am certainly open to it. Also, if you see something that makes you think: this is going wrong, or this could be better in the animal(free) research field, please let me know and we will look at it together. I am mainly concerned with stakeholder engagement, so if people within the work packages feel that they are still missing stakeholders, or if they see any opportunities, I would love to get in touch with you. You can reach me at Waard@zonmw.nl.
