On Tuesday, November 5, the VHP4Safety consortium gathered in Wageningen for the project update meeting discussing the progress and path forward. With 1.5 years remaining in this 5-year initiative, the day provided an opportunity for all consortium members, including project partners, PhD students, postdocs, collaboration and cooperation partners, become updated and to review milestones reached and interact to collaboratively outline the steps needed to complete our ambitious goals. Through interactive sessions,, consortium members aligned on progress across research lines, addressing what has been achieved and the work that lies ahead to bring the Virtual Human Platform to its full potential.

The day began with an introduction by a welcome by Nynke Kramer, the WUR researcher involved in our project who kindly welcomed the VHP4Safety consortium at the Wageningen venue. This was followed by an introduction by the coordination team, presenting todays program and inviting all consortium members to engage in interactive discussions throughout the day.

Research Line 1 on building the VHP kicked off the program with updates from WP1.1 on the ICT infrastructure,WP1.2 on toxicokinetics, and WP1.3 on in silico modeling, spotlighting the current state of platform integration (WP1.1), developments in physiologically based kinetic modeling and the integration in the project (WP1.2), and state of development of in silico tools and services on the platform (WP1.3). Interactive discussion with the consortium informed the work packages on how to improve the data alignment across the various tools, their integration on the platform and their connection and use within Reerach Line 2 and 3.

After a coffee break, Research Line 3 on implementation of the VHP shared progress in WP3.1 on technology assessment, WP3.2 on acceptance and WP3.3 on training and education, focusing on the societal aspect of the development and implementation of the VHP. The work on impact practices and user research was presented (WP3.1) and the work on the innovation framework and building trust towards acceptance of the VHP (WP3.2). WP3.3 presented the work on training and education, including all the courses and programmes that have been developed and are planned.

During a short intermezzo, a special guest, Iryna Popova, was invited to provide a presentation on her research on thyroid hormone disruption. Iryna is a Ukrainian guest researcher from the Bukovnian State Medical University, who received the NWO Hop-On funding. This funding allows to collaborate with the thyroid case of VHP4Safety as well as with AFARA, the VHP4Safety sister project, to continue her scientific career under the difficult condition she is in.

Following lunch, the session resumed with Research Line 2, on feeding the VHP, which presented advancements across the work packages, WP2.1 on in vitro models for toxicodynamics, WP2.2 on kidney function failure, WP2.3 on neurodegeneration and WP2.4 on thyroid-mediated brain development. In the WP2.1 session, the data from the in vitro experiments to inform the toxicokinetics in WP1.2 were discussed. The WP2.2 to 2.3 presentations focused on the AOPs developed, in vitro studies performed and workflows established in the case studies, as well as on the path forward to connect the workflows to the platform.

In the final session, the progress in the VHP4Safety Task Forces (TFs), set up at the start of the project to integrate activities between work packages, was presented The TF Common Language presented their work on creating the VHP4Safety glossary (glossary.vhp4safety.nl). The TF AOP Development shared their progress on developing the AOPs, stating that the task force has now become redundant, since the connections between different work packages has been established and sustained. The TF Resource Output Management (previously called Data Management) presented their ongoing efforts to improve data sharing across partners. The TF Risk Assessment provided an update on the link of the regulatory questions to the workflows of the three different case studies and how this could be connected to overarching workflows, such as the ASPA framework. Lastly, the TF Sustainability presented how to ensure that the VHP will be sustained after the VHP4Safety projects’ end.

The meeting concluded with closing remarks from the overall coordinator Juliette Legler, followed by an informal networking session with drinks, allowing participants to exchange further ideas and strengthen connections.
