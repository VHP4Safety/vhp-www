The future of toxicology took a giant leap forward at our recent VHP4Safety Hackathon, where brilliant minds converged to tackle the challenge of integrating artificial intelligence into chemical safety assessment. This two-day event wasn't just successful—it was transformative, setting new benchmarks for innovation and collaboration in our field.

A Convergence of Minds and Technologies
From the outset, our goal was ambitious: to identify how chemical safety assessment can benefit by using AI-driven technologies. The response from our participants was nothing short of extraordinary. For the first time in the history of our hackathons, every team emerged victorious, delivering outstanding results across all five challenge areas!

Key Achievements:

  1. BioCypher Implementation: Teams successfully tested the BioCypher framework, creating knowledge graphs for thyroid and Parkinson's case studies. This breakthrough allows for natural language querying of complex scientific data.
  2. LLM-Powered Agents: Participants developed an array of useful AI agents, including tools for QSAR training, RNA-Seq data analysis, and toxicity prediction. These agents promise to streamline and enhance various aspects of toxicological research.
  3. In Vitro Model Selection Framework: A novel approach for assessing in vitro models was developed, complete with scoring and weighting criteria. This lays the groundwork for AI-assisted selection of appropriate models for regulatory and scientific purposes.
  4. Next-Gen Risk Assessment Workflow: Multiple workflows were mapped out to address regulatory questions related to thyroid studies, significantly advancing our understanding of workflow automation in risk assessment.
  5. LLM-Assisted Data Template Population: A proof-of-concept RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation) system was created, optimizing the extraction and summarization of data from scientific documents.

The Power of Preparation
The hackathon's success was built on a foundation of knowledge-sharing. Three preparatory webinars set the stage:

  1. An introduction to integrating Large Language Models (LLMs) and Knowledge Graphs into toxicology workflows.
  2. A deep dive into the BioCypher framework, led by Marvin Martens and our guest speaker Sebastian Lobentanzer from Heidelberg University.
  3. An overview of the TempTox challenge and thyroid case study workflow, presented by our own experts.

These sessions armed participants with the context and tools needed to hit the ground running.

A Team Effort
The hackathon's success is a testament to the power of collaborative, interdisciplinary work. Special recognition goes to the organizational team—Ivo Djidrovski, Marvin Martens, Ozan Cinar, Jente Houweling, Marc Teunis, and Juliette Legler—whose efforts made this event possible. We're also grateful to Sebastian Lobentanzer for his leadership in our BioCypher work, and to Rob Beffers for his invaluable support with LLM notebooks and technical expertise.

Looking Ahead
The hackathon's outcomes mark a significant step forward in integrating AI technologies into toxicology workflows. The tools and frameworks developed, such as the BioCypher knowledge graphs and LLM-powered agents, will continue to be refined and tested for broader application in chemical safety assessments. Our focus moving forward will be on validating these innovations, improving their accuracy, and ensuring they meet regulatory requirements. This work will support the development of more efficient and precise methods for toxicological risk assessment. We remain committed to advancing these efforts and will provide regular updates as progress is made.
