VHP4Safety's sister project AFARA was launched at the Utrecht University Hall. The goal of the project “Animal-Free Assays for endocrine disruption: from science to Regulatory Acceptance” (AFARA) is to facilitate the pathways for regulatory acceptance of existing models for endocrine disruption that are both animal-free and human-relevant.











Creating human-relevant testing strategies

Prof. dr. Juliette Legler: “Endocrine disrupting chemicals (EDCs) are increasingly linked to various human diseases acting via the endocrine system. That's why protection of public health from the potentially harmful effects of exposure to EDCs requires human-relevant testing strategies.”

Despite the societal and political urgency to reduce animal testing, current testing strategies for assessing the endocrine disrupting properties of chemicals rely on resource-intensive and invasive animal models. Although these animal models are ethically debated and limited due to the challenges of translating effects in rodents to the human situation, the development of animal-free alternatives faces serious hurdles to move along the path towards regulatory acceptance, including processes of standardization and validation.

“Within the project we are combining academic expertise across the natural sciences, social sciences and humanities with key public sector expertise on EDC regulation” states Jarno Hoekman of the Copernicus Institute of Sustainable Development. “It’s fantastic that we work together with the industry, NGOs, regulatory authorities and model developers to achieve better protection of citizens from EDC-related harm and reduce the use of animal testing.”
