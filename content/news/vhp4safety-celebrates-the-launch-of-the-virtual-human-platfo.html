On Thursday 28 May, the launch of the Virtual Human Platform (VHP) took place. The event, held at the historic venue Zalen van Zeven, started with a welcome and introduction by the VHP4Safety coordination team. They looked back on the kick-off of the VHP4Safety project, which took place during the COVID-19 pandemic, and showed the "Let's Get Started" video that was created for that occasion.

The coordination team also reflected on the project's collaborative way of working and highlighted how the platform was developed through contributions from experts across different disciplines, with input from a wide range of stakeholders. They then introduced the Virtual Human Platform and, after a 10-second countdown, officially launched the VHP.

Next, Fabian Wagenaars provided an introduction to the platform, starting with a tour of the website. He showed participants where to find the tools, in vitro methods, datasets, and the safety assessment workflow. He also demonstrated how these components can be combined to answer regulatory questions, using examples from the VHP4Safety case studies.

Following this introduction, Fabian presented the Thyroid Hormone case study. He demonstrated how the question, "Does silychristin influence thyroid hormone-mediated brain development in the fetus, resulting in cognitive impairment in children?" was addressed by following the different steps of the safety assessment workflow.

The demonstration was followed by three breakout sessions. During the Data, Models, and AI session, participants explored the technical aspects of the platform and attended pitch presentations on several of the available tools. In the From Petri Dish to Platform session, participants learned more about the Parkinson's case study and received demonstrations on how to make their in vitro data available through the platform. In the Breaking Barriers session, participants explored mission-specific innovation systems and discussed how the development of the VHP fits within this framework.

After a short coffee break, the pitch session began. The first three pitches provided summaries of the breakout sessions, followed by presentations from the people hosting stands at the information market. Afterwards, the information market opened, offering participants an interactive environment with plenty of opportunities to exchange knowledge, experiences, and perspectives. For many attendees, this was one of the highlights of the day.

Overall, the launch was a successful event that showcased the Virtual Human Platform and its potential to support human-relevant safety assessment. We hope participants will continue to explore and use the platform in their future safety assessment activities.
