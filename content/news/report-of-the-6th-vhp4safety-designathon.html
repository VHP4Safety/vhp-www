The programme began with an update from Anne Kienhuis on the progress within all Work Packages and Research Lines. This was followed by an introduction to agile working and the SCRUM methodology by Frank Hepkema. Since November, Research Line 1 has adopted agile methods to accelerate platform development, and this interactive session helped clarify how the SCRUM process contributes to delivering a usable final product.

Anne also shared the first draft of a description of the final platform output:

“By the end of the VHP4Safety project, we will present the virtual human platform and demonstrate how each case study addresses at least one regulatory question. This will be based on the workflow, associated modules, and data, all integrated into a user-friendly interface designed for use by relevant stakeholders.”

Participants provided feedback on this description via post-its, which will be used by the Coordination Team to refine the formulation.

The group then took part in a session titled “Prioritising the work towards the end of the project.” Each Research Line identified three key priorities to focus on during the final project phase:

Research Line 1

  • Make all tools and data available on the platform

  • Integrate tools to support answering regulatory questions

  • Develop a user-friendly interface

Research Line 2

  • Finalise data collection and store it in validated formats

  • Bring data to the platform

  • Translate case study workflows onto the platform

Research Line 3

  • Focus on outreach and awareness for regulators and industry

  • Define criteria for platform acceptance

  • Finalise training materials and tutorials related to case studies

These priorities, along with concrete steps per Research Line, have been summarised and shared on Teams.

In the afternoon, the consortium was joined virtually by Pavlo Zadorozhnii, who is working on the project from Ukraine through an NWO hop-on grant. Pavlo presented his work on computational models to predict thyroid toxicity, developed together with Linde Schoenmaker and Gerard van Westen.

The day concluded with a World Café session on “A first look at the final platform,” organised by Research Lines 1 and 3. Participants explored early versions of the website’s landing page, navigation menu, workflows, and other key elements, providing feedback to improve the design and user experience. The day ended with a well-earned drink and a delicious Italian buffet.

On Friday morning, Cyrille Krul opened the second day with a recap. Next, Menno Brandjes and Inés Beekers introduced participants to ORTEC’s LogicSuite.

Participants then joined one of three parallel workshops:

  • A Masterclass Innovation System Approach by Simona Negro, aimed at identifying key stakeholders and interventions to accelerate the transition to animal-free safety assessment

  • A clinical data workshop led by ORTEC, focused on the use of clinical datasets in the kidney and thyroid case studies

  • A tutorial video workshop hosted by Mirthe and Aniek, where participants learned how to script and structure tool tutorials

In the afternoon, Isaac Ortega Alvarado, Cyrille Krul and Jarno Hoekman led a session on the sustainability of the Virtual Human Platform. The group discussed what knowledge, tools and relationships should be preserved beyond the end of the project, and what is needed to ensure long-term continuity. This input will feed into the VHP4Safety Sustainability Plan.

The meeting concluded with a wrap-up by Juliette Legler — and of course, a final round of drinks.

A big thank you to KWR for hosting us in such a welcoming venue, and to all participants for their valuable contributions. We look forward to continuing the work toward our shared goal in the coming months!
